HCD

For this project I had to make a website that was only usefull for one person, this person is Darice. She became deaf at a later stage in her life, she wanted a website that would make her enjoy watching films more. I ran several rounds of testing with Darice, who ended up playing the biggest role in sharpening my concept. The first tests made it clear that my ideas were still too abstract and that I was focusing too much on color and visual effects. Her main feedback was that clarity, calmness and being able to follow who's speaking matter way more than decorative elements.

Based on that feedback I adjusted the design step by step. Colors became less dominant and more supportive, while I put more emphasis on subtitles, speaker indication and subtle animations that support the content instead of distracting from it.

The end result is an interface that's less experimental and visually busy, but fits her needs much better: clear, readable and easy to follow. The feel now comes not from standalone effects but from small, subtle interactions that strengthen the experience without disrupting the text.

Meesterproef

As the final project of the Web Design and Development minor, I worked with a team to build a digital learning platform for Stichting Mensenkenners, an organization that trains staff in (semi-)public spaces to interact more inclusively with people with disabilities. 8 out of 10 interactions in public spaces feel uncomfortable for someone with a disability, not because of physical barriers, but because of social awkwardness.

We turned their existing in-person training into a self-paced online platform with a personal dashboard, interactive modules on awareness, perspective and accessibility, a disability simulator and progress tracking. It's built with HTML, CSS and JavaScript in Astro, Supabase for authentication and the database, and a custom API for the course content.
